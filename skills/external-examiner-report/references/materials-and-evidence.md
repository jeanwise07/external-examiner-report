# Materials and evidence

## Intake

Inspect the supplied directory first. Ask one focused question for missing locations, academic year or special institutional requirements. If focus courses are not specified, identify and show the course inventory before asking the user to choose; do not expect them to recall course names before you inspect the materials. Avoid separate questions for every attachment and repeated permission requests.

| Material | Purpose | Limit if missing |
|---|---|---|
| Embedded Writing Style, blueprint and examples | Voice, depth and question-by-question structure | Self-contained; no old report is required. Follow-up judgments require verified submission or response records. |
| Bundled base proforma plus any current form, guidance or new requirements | Required questions, institutional layout and scope | Use the bundled base form when no newer edition is supplied; disclose its September 2024 date and do not claim current-year confirmation. |
| Programme outcomes, regulations, structure and descriptions | Curriculum and coherence | Two courses alone cannot establish whole-programme design. |
| Selected course outlines, tasks, examination questions/answers, rubrics and weights | Content and assessment alignment | Without marking criteria, limit strong conclusions about grading fairness. |
| Complete marked work across supplied high/middle/low bands, reports and presentations | Demonstrated ability, grading and feedback | Record missing bands; do not infer they do not exist. |
| Representative teaching materials and learning support arrangements | Teaching design and research integration | Plans do not prove classroom effectiveness. |
| Course and component results, with comparable historical aggregates if available | Distributions, overall performance and trends | Selected bands cannot estimate cohort grade distributions. |
| Assessed capstone work, individual assessment and outcome summaries | Integration and graduating-cohort outcomes | A capstone brief does not demonstrate attainment by all graduates. |
| Comments received by students, completed rubrics and moderation records | Feedback and implementation | A blank rubric does not prove feedback was delivered. |
| Institutional responses and evidence following previous review | Improvement and follow-up | No response means implementation cannot be confirmed, not that no improvement occurred. |

Use existing evidence first; do not automatically require the institution to create complex new datasets.

## Proportionate reading and records

If sampling is unspecified, propose a transparent, reproducible approach suited to volume. One possible approach is to read all supplied project reports and presentation samples for the selected courses, plus the first and last complete quiz/midterm submissions in each available high/middle/low band, or all when fewer than two exist. This is adjustable, not a permanent rule or guarantee of statistical representativeness. Expand targeted checks when an inconsistency emerges.

Record the source file, original PDF physical page, printed page if different, sample scope, actual reading, observation, supported conclusion and limitation. Use anonymous sample labels; do not copy student identities into this skill or the formal report.

Keep three levels separate:

1. Programme/course design: whether content, tasks and criteria provide appropriate learning opportunities.
2. Demonstrated sample achievement: what actual reviewed work shows.
3. Graduating-cohort programme achievement: needs broader course/capstone evidence and aggregate results; two-course samples alone are insufficient.

Not seeing feedback records is not proof of no feedback. A peer-assessment mechanism is not proof of effective use across all courses. A task requiring an outcome is not proof students achieved it. Separate curriculum-standard judgments from comparisons of actual student attainment; do not assert equivalence with named universities without comparable evidence.

Respect the actual task: a permitted literature-based project is not deficient simply because it lacks a backtest. A well-supported negative result is not poor work simply because no successful strategy was found.

## Choose useful recommendations

Prioritize material effects on learning, reliable assessment and curriculum connections. Use enough evidence to justify a judgment; keep the diagnostic detail in working records. Low-impact typos, an isolated arithmetic mistake and an unexpanded grading dispute should not crowd out the principal recommendations.

Avoid generalization. If some courses already assess individual contribution, recognize that practice and identify where sharing or implementation evidence is needed. Fit suggestions into existing teaching where possible rather than automatically adding a large assessment or administrative burden.
